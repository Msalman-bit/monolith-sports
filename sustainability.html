Sialkot's football industry was the subject of one of the most significant child labour interventions in manufacturing history. We were a signatory, and we have been monitored ever since.

01 — Labour

The Atlanta Agreement
is not history to us.

In 1997 the ILO, UNICEF and the Sialkot Chamber of Commerce agreed a programme to remove children from football stitching. The mechanism was simple and severe: stitching moved out of unmonitored homes and into registered centres that inspectors could enter without warning.

We signed in 1998. Every one of our forty-one centres is registered, geo-located and subject to unannounced inspection by the Independent Monitoring Association for Child Labour. Any ball we make carries the code of the centre that stitched it.

The honest version: the industry's problem was never that manufacturers wanted children on the needle. It was that piecework in private homes is invisible. Registered centres solved the visibility problem, and visibility is what makes the commitment real.

02 — Environment

Measured, reduced,
reported annually.

Sialkot sits in a water-stressed district with an unreliable grid. Environmental investment here is as much operational necessity as ethics.

Energy

1.1 MW rooftop solar

Installed across both units in 2022 and 2024. Covers 46 % of annual consumption and almost all daytime load in summer, which also removes our dependence on diesel generation during load shedding.

Water

Closed-loop treatment

Latex dipping and dyeing effluent passes through a three-stage plant and returns to process. Discharge to municipal sewer fell 71 % between 2019 and 2025. No untreated effluent leaves either site.

Waste

Offcut recovery

Panel and leather offcuts are baled and sold to local shoe and glove trades rather than landfilled. Foam scrap is shredded into punch bag fill. Landfill diversion sits at 88 % by weight.

Materials

GRS and FSC lines

Recycled polyester linings and packaging board are certified to the Global Recycled Standard. Cane, willow and cartonboard carry FSC chain of custody. Chrome-free tanned leather is available on every leather line.

04 — Questions buyers ask

The uncomfortable ones

Because it is a registered building with a fixed address, not a private home. Each centre has a posted registration number, an attendance register, and it is inspected without notice by IMAC monitors who are not paid by us. Every ball is coded to its centre, so a finding can be traced to a specific location and a specific supervisor.

The honest limit: monitoring proves the absence of children in the centre during inspections. It does not prove anything about work that never enters the system. That is exactly why we do not permit any home-based subcontracting, and why our terms make it a termination event.

Our lowest grade sits at 127 % of the Punjab statutory minimum. Against the Global Living Wage Coalition benchmark for rural Punjab, that is roughly at parity, not above it. We are not going to claim we have solved living wages. We publish the gap, we have closed it by eleven percentage points since 2021, and we are happy to build a costed plan into a long-term programme with a buyer who wants to fund closing the rest.
